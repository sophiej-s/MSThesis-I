### Spelling Autocorrection Libraries:
* autocorrect
* textblob
* pyspellchecker

An initial test was on an arbitrary misspelled phrase *"Thsi toaster dos nt cos much"* (the intended sentence is "This toaster does not cost much"):
* autocorrect:  "This coaster dos nt cos much" (thsi->this; toaster->coaster; "dos", "nt" and "cos" are not autocorrected)
* textblob: "His master do it cos much" (this->his; toaster->master; nt->it; dos->do; "cos" is not autocorrected)
* pyspellchecker: "his toaster dos it cos much" (this->his; the "toaster" is finally unchanged; "dos", "nt" and "cos" are not autocorrected)

The pyspellchecker made fewest autocorrections while textblob made the most autocorrections in the sentence. 


Looking at reviews / examples from the Toys dataset:

(1) Example 1:
* Processed: hem costume began unraveling almost immediately worn cheaply made item material poor quality would recommend dress anyone
* autocorrect: hem costume began traveling almost immediately worn cheaply made item material poor quality would recommend dress anyone  (unraveling->traveling (wrong correction)
* textblob: hem costume began unravelling almost immediately worn cheaply made item material poor quality would recommend dress anyone (unravelling is kept unchanged)
* pyspellchecker: hem costume began unraveling almost immediately worn cheaply made item material poor quality would recommend dress anyone (no correction)


(2) Example 2:
* Processed review: got three thing really mean get ten frickin dollar waste money
* autocorrect: got three thing really mean get ten friction dollar waste money (friction / wrong guess)
* textblob: got three thing really mean get ten friction dollar waste money (friction / wrong guess)
* pyspellchecker: got three thing really mean get ten frickin dollar waste money (no correction)


**CONCLUSION: No clear winner. Not currently adopted in the design.**
