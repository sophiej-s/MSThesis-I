### 
Text2Emotion at https://pypi.org/project/text2emotion/ rates texts on five types of emotions.

````
For example, the following results made sense:

text = "Day was pretty amazing😃😃"
te.get_emotion(text)
# Out[65]: {'Happy': 1.0, 'Angry': 0.0, 'Surprise': 0.0, 'Sad': 0.0, 'Fear': 0.0}


text="'Great quality wooden track (better than some others we have tried). Perfect match to the various vintages of Thomas track that we already have. There is enough track here to have fun and get creative incorporating your key pieces with track splits, loops and bends.'"
te.get_emotion(text)
# Out[70]: {'Happy': 0.2, 'Angry': 0.2, 'Surprise': 0.2, 'Sad': 0.4, 'Fear': 0.0}
`````

While other did not make sense:
`````````
te.get_emotion("This is unacceptable")
# Out[63]: {'Happy': 0, 'Angry': 0, 'Surprise': 0, 'Sad': 0, 'Fear': 0}
``````````````


Test:
Toys dataset. 10 K samples x 2 (ratings of 1 and 5) were transformed using the Text2Emotion.


Histograms showing 0.1 prediction bins (rounded to 1 digit, i.e., 0.32->0.3) for each of the emotions on the reviews for ratings of 1. 
While the results show that there were more angry reviews (as expected), all of the reviews contained small scores / small amounts of each of the emotions. My expectation was that there will be a clearer divergence between the "happy" vs  "angry" and "sad".

![df-1_10K samples](https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/20401990/163505451-52275ae9-b144-4fa7-819c-0ad73a5b1af7.png)


Histograms showing 0.1 prediction bins (rounded to 1 digit, i.e., 0.32->0.3) for each of the emotions on the reviews for ratings of 5. 
These are reviews from happy customers, and the extectation was that there would be more higher "happy" scores.
![Df-5-10K](https://user-images.githubusercontent.com/20401990/163505454-1e9c7eb3-14d0-4ac9-ac11-d85475fec310.png)
